Market-data provider summary (YAHOO), not the 10-K Item 1

Microsoft Corporation, a technology company, develops and supports a portfolio of technology solutions for individuals and businesses worldwide. Its products include operating systems, server applications, business solution applications, software development tools, desktop and server management tools, and video games; and devices, such as PCs, tablets, gaming and entertainment consoles, other intelligent devices, and related accessories. The company's Productivity and Business Processes segment offers Microsoft 365 Commercial, Enterprise Mobility + Security, Power BI, Exchange, SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, Microsoft 365 Security and Compliance, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Windows Commercial on-premises and Office licensed on-premises. This segment also provides Microsoft 365 Consumer products and cloud services; LinkedIn, including talent solutions, marketing solutions, subscriptions, and sales solutions; Dynamics 365, a set of cloud-based applications; and on-premises ERP and CRM applications. Its Intelligent Cloud segment offers server products and cloud services; cloud and AI consumption-based services, GitHub cloud services, health and life sciences cloud services, as well as virtual desktop offerings, and other cloud services; SQL Server, Windows Server, Visual Studio, System Center, and related client access licenses; and enterprise support services, industry solutions, Microsoft partner network, and learning experience. The company's More Personal Computing segment provides Windows OEM licensing and devices comprising Surface and PC accessories; XBOX hardware, and XBOX first- and third-party content and services; XBOX Game Pass and other subscriptions; XBOX Cloud Gaming, advertising, and other cloud services; and search advertising consisting of Bing, Copilot, Microsoft News, Microsoft Edge, and third-party affiliates. It sells its products through partners and retail networks. The company was founded in 1975 and is headquartered in Redmond, Washington.

Risks & Thesis

Bear case

AI and cloud demand normalises faster than the current build-out assumes. Azure growth decelerates from the 43% posted in the June 2026 quarter toward the rate of the broader commercial base, Windows OEM, devices and Xbox keep shrinking, and revenue growth settles near the weakest year in the filed decade (6.9% in FY2023). The enormous capital programme — capex of $115.9bn in FY2026 against $34.3bn of depreciation — lands in the cost base as depreciation catches up, so operating margin gives back the recent gains and runs below the ten-year median rather than at the FY2026 peak of 46.8%. New capital earns roughly the discount rate: filed ROIC has already fallen from 94.0% in FY2018 to 26.0% in FY2026 as invested capital scaled, and the five-year incremental return of 17.3% compresses further in a capital-heavy AI regime. Rising stock-based compensation outpaces repurchases and share count drifts up slightly.

Base case

Microsoft compounds off a very large base at a rate between its ten-year CAGR (14.7%) and the slowest year on file. Commercial remaining performance obligation of $684bn at FY2026 year-end, up from $375bn a year earlier, plus Microsoft 365 Copilot at over 30 million paid seats and Azure crossing $100bn of annual revenue, underpin mid-teens growth that fades as the backlog converts. Operating margin holds close to the FY2026 level of 46.8% — mix shifts toward capital-intensive cloud and AI infrastructure, which carries more depreciation, but software and M365 scale offsets it — so the base sits just below the peak at 45.5%. Returns on new capital stay well above the cost of capital but far below the asset-light history: 18% is near the trailing five-year incremental return of 17.3% and well under the 26.0% FY2026 ROIC. Net reinvestment stays elevated versus the 6.2% median of capex less depreciation as data-centre spend continues, and steady repurchases shrink the share count modestly.

Bull case

The agentic-computing cycle keeps Microsoft's growth at the top of its filed range. Revenue grew 18% in FY2026 and 17.7% in the June quarter, Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 27% to $59.3bn, Azure grew 43%, and the AI business passed a $37bn run rate up 123% year over year; commercial RPO up 84% to $678bn gives multi-year visibility. Growth holds at 16% for five years. The AI stack proves high-margin as utilisation of the FY2024–FY2026 capex wave rises, pushing operating margin above the FY2026 record to 48%. New capital earns 22% after tax — above the recent five-year incremental return of 17.3% but still below the 26.0% ROIC Microsoft actually filed in FY2026 — and strong free cash flow (operating cash flow $182.9bn in FY2026) funds both the build-out and continued repurchases, lowering the share count.
